# Patient Safety and Quality Assurance

## How is patient identification verified?

Patient identity must be verified using two identifiers (Full Name and Date of Birth or Patient ID number) against the wristband before any medication, blood test, or procedure.

## What are the high-alert medications at Nawaloka?

High-alert medications include Insulin, Opiates/Narcotics, Injectable Potassium Chloride/Phosphate, Intravenous Anticoagulants (Heparin), and Chemotherapeutic agents. These require independent double-checks.

## What is the fall prevention programme?

All admitted patients undergo a Morse Fall Scale assessment. High-risk patients receive a yellow wristband, bed alarms are activated, and non-slip footwear is provided.

## How are medication errors reported?

Medication errors must be reported immediately to the attending physician to manage patient safety, followed by an incident report submitted via the internal portal within 24 hours for root-cause analysis.

## What is the Braden Scale for pressure injury risk?

The Braden Scale assesses a patient's risk of developing pressure ulcers based on sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition, and friction/shear. Scores under 18 trigger preventive protocols.

## How does incident reporting work?

Any adverse event, near miss, or hazard must be documented in the online Incident Reporting System. Reports are non-punitive and reviewed by the Quality Assurance committee to improve systemic safety.
