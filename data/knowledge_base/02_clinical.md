# Clinical Protocols and Patient Care

## What is the patient admission process?

Patients arrive via OPD, Emergency, or planned admission. The admission desk verifies identity and insurance, initiates the EMR file, and escorts the patient to the ward, where nursing staff perform the initial assessment.

## How does the triage system work in the emergency department?

The Emergency Department uses a 5-level triage system: Resuscitation (immediate), Emergent (within 15 mins), Urgent (within 30 mins), Less Urgent (within 1 hr), and Non-Urgent (within 2 hrs).

## What is the ISBAR handover protocol?

All clinical handovers must follow the ISBAR format: Identify (patient details), Situation (current problem), Background (brief history), Assessment (current vital signs/findings), and Recommendation (what needs to be done).

## What is the discharge process for inpatients?

Discharge is initiated by the consultant's written order. Nursing staff prepares a discharge summary, pharmacy prepares take-home medications, and billing finalizing the invoice. Patients are briefed on post-discharge care before leaving.

## What are the five rights of medication administration?

Nurses must verify the Five Rights before administering any medication: Right Patient, Right Drug, Right Dose, Right Route, and Right Time.

## What is the surgical safety checklist?

The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist is mandatory for all procedures. It involves three stages: Sign-in (before induction of anesthesia), Time-out (before skin incision), and Sign-out (before the patient leaves the operating room).

## What is the blood transfusion protocol?

Blood transfusions require two RNs to independently verify patient ID, blood type, and component expiry against the doctor's order at the bedside before starting the transfusion. Vitals must be monitored at 15 mins, 30 mins, and hourly.
